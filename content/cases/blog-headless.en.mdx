## Context

Flexicar's blog lived at `blog.flexicar.es`, a subdomain separate from the main domain `flexicar.es`. For Google, that was essentially a different website: 1,000+ articles accumulated over years contributed no authority to the root domain.

The editorial team also relied on a legacy system with no support for rich blocks or accessibility optimizations.

## Problem

- **SEO authority dilution**: backlinks pointed to the subdomain, not the main domain.
- **Potential duplicate content**: similar URLs across two domains without coherent canonicals.
- **Editorial bottleneck**: without structured blocks, Marketing couldn't independently publish quality content (comparison tables, FAQs, CTAs, etc.).
- **Accessibility debt**: the previous system did not apply WCAG 2.1 AA.

## Action

I designed and coordinated the full migration in three phases:

### Phase 1 — Architecture & spec

- Defined 13 editorial block types (paragraph, image, table, FAQ, CTA, video embed, code, callout, comparison, timeline, testimonial, gallery, product block).
- Schema.org specification by content type: `Article`, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`.
- 301 redirect rules at Nginx level for every URL in the old blog.
- Accessibility criteria: mandatory alt text on images, minimum contrast ratios, heading hierarchy.

### Phase 2 — URL migration

- Full inventory extraction script: 1,092 URLs categorized.
- Source → destination mapping audited by SEO and Editorial teams.
- Individual cannibalization review for the 50 highest-traffic URLs.
- Redirect QA in staging before production activation.

### Phase 3 — Go-live & monitoring

- Staged activation by category (not a big bang launch).
- Search Console tracking dashboard for 30 days post-migration.
- Fast-response protocol for URLs with impression drops.

## Impact

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| URLs migrated | 1,092 |
| Editorial block types | 13 |
| Organic traffic post-migration | 100% preserved |
| SERP stability window | 30 days monitored |

The subdomain's SEO authority consolidated into the main domain. The editorial team gained full publishing autonomy using predefined templates.
