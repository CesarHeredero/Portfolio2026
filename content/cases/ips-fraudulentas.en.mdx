## Context

During a routine Google Search Console review we detected an anomalous pattern: hundreds of new backlinks pointing to Flexicar pages from unrecognizable domains. Analysis revealed that 300+ unique IPs were generating artificial links in an automated fashion.

## Problem

- **Penalty risk**: Google can interpret artificial link patterns as deliberate PageRank manipulation, even if the beneficiary didn't request them.
- **Link profile contamination**: the disavow file was outdated and didn't cover the new domains.
- **No established protocol**: the team had no incident response procedure for this type of SEO attack.
- **Exposure window**: the more pages Google crawled with those links, the higher the risk.

## Action

### Phase 1 — Audit & classification

- Full backlink profile extraction from Search Console and Ahrefs.
- Domain classification: clear spam, suspicious, legitimate.
- Identification of 300+ unique IPs involved and source URL patterns.
- Prioritization by link volume and spam domain age.

### Phase 2 — Infrastructure-level blocking

- Coordination with the Infra team for WAF (Web Application Firewall) rules.
- IP range blocking by ASN when the pattern was clear enough.
- Monitoring of new emerging IPs for 2 weeks.

### Phase 3 — Google cleanup

- Disavow file update in Search Console with all identified domains.
- Protocol documentation for future incidents.
- Briefing to the SEO team on early warning signals.

## Impact

Flexicar's domain authority was protected. The documented response protocol became the SEO team's standard procedure for similar incidents.

No traffic data directly attributable to the block (the negative effects never materialized), but the risk was neutralized before Google processed the links in its next index update.
