## Context

Safari ITP (Intelligent Tracking Prevention) deletes third-party cookies 7 days after the last visit. In a car ecommerce where the decision cycle spans weeks or months, this means losing track of users at exactly the critical moment.

On top of that, 28% of active Flexicar users were blocking or rejecting client-side tracking.

## Problem

- **Analytics blindness**: nearly a third of users were invisible to GA4 and Meta Pixel.
- **Broken attribution**: conversions arriving months later couldn't be linked to the original visit.
- **Disconnected CRM data**: Salesforce leads weren't cross-referenced with web behavior.
- **Legal pressure**: GDPR required a more robust consent model for compliance.

## Action

### Server-side architecture

- Google Tag Manager Server-Side deployment on own infrastructure (Cloud Run).
- First-party cookies set from the server: 2-year lifetime, own domain.
- GA4 and Meta CAPI event routing through the server before reaching final destinations.

### CRM and BigQuery integration

- Technical specification of the data flow: web → sGTM → BigQuery → Salesforce.
- Identity model definition: unified `client_id`, `user_id`, `session_id`.
- Reconciliation dashboard comparing client-side vs server-side data.

### Team coordination

I coordinated 4 teams in parallel over 3 months:
- **Infra**: Cloud Run provisioning, sGTM domain setup.
- **Dev**: dataLayer modifications and custom events.
- **Data**: BigQuery table design and pipelines.
- **Legal/Compliance**: consent model review and cookie policy.

## Impact

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Visible users | ~72% | ~100% |
| Cookie lifetime (Safari) | 7 days | 2 years |
| Multi-session attribution | Broken | Working |
| Teams coordinated | — | 4 |

Paid media investment became measurable for a previously invisible segment, enabling budget allocation optimization based on real data.
